# Commerce Payment Reversals Audit

Status: **IMPLEMENTED — CI REQUIRED BEFORE MERGE**

## Evidence and deliberate limits
The current Commerce checkpoint requires a focused refunds/chargebacks + entitlement/revenue reversal slice and explicitly forbids inventing refund eligibility, partial-refund formulas, provider-fee recovery, payout clawback policy, or irreversible provider operations.

Tap documentation distinguishes full and partial refunds and marks `REFUNDED` as the finalized successful state that triggers the configured webhook. Tap payout documentation also treats refunds and disputes as payout-reducing facts and notes that a dispute may later resolve as a chargeback.

The product blueprint/source material does not define how a partial refund changes access, trainer allocation, provider fee recovery, or package/Assessment consumption. Therefore this batch implements only factual **full** reversals.

## What this slice does
- adds normalized `commerce_payment_reversals`.
- one full reversal per PaymentRequest.
- reversal types: `refund` and `chargeback`.
- factual sources: verified provider webhook or platform-admin external evidence.
- exact trusted paid amount and currency are mandatory.
- PaymentRequest moves from `paid` to `refunded` or `chargeback`.
- active Entitlements generated by that PaymentRequest are revoked atomically.
- factual revenue row is marked reversed with amount/reference/time.
- historical allocation and payout evidence is preserved rather than rewritten.
- once revenue is reversed, new allocation or trainer-payout marking is rejected.

## What this slice does not do
- it does not call Tap's refund API.
- it does not decide whether a customer is eligible for a refund.
- it does not support partial refunds.
- it does not restore a consumed discount redemption.
- it does not automatically recover provider fees.
- it does not claw back money already paid to a trainer.
- it does not infer a chargeback from a normal failed/cancelled payment event.

These are deliberate UNKNOWN/policy boundaries rather than missing implementation.

## Tap refund webhook
The existing Tap endpoint now accepts both Charge and Refund objects.

For Refund:
- raw body is retained for verification/evidence hash.
- Tap `hashstring` is recomputed from canonical refund id/amount/currency/gateway-reference/payment-reference/status/created fields.
- only final `REFUNDED` is mapped to a Commerce refund.
- `ACCEPTED`, pending, failed, rejected and other non-final refund states do not revoke access.
- internal PaymentRequest correlation uses the nested original Charge `reference.order` when present and falls back to the immutable Tap charge/session ID.
- the refund amount must equal the server-authoritative PaymentRequest final amount.

## Generic provider reversal
The existing signed generic provider event contract now also accepts `refunded` and `chargeback` statuses. The same provider/event idempotency, payload hash, provider match, exact amount and exact currency checks apply.

## Admin evidence path
A platform admin can record an externally completed full refund or chargeback when reconciliation evidence comes from a dashboard/report rather than a live webhook.

The command requires:
- exact optimistic PaymentRequest revision.
- explicit `refund|chargeback` type.
- provider/external reference.
- evidence text.
- CSRF + platform-admin role.

It records the fact only. It does not execute a provider-side money movement.

## Revenue/payout truth
A reversal adds explicit fields to the factual revenue entry:
- reversal type.
- full reversed amount.
- external/provider reference.
- reversed timestamp.

Prior settlement and payout records remain historical facts. If trainer payout was already marked paid, it stays paid and the reversal is visible alongside it. No automatic clawback is invented. If payout/allocation was not yet recorded, further allocation/payout mutation is blocked after reversal.

## Discount semantics
A discount that was already redeemed by the paid sale remains redeemed. Returning coupon capacity after a refund is not source-defined and is not inferred.
